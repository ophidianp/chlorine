Keep the details. Spend less time waiting.

From distant mob AI to bursts of particles and sounds, Chlorine offers small, configurable adjustments to the work that can add up during play.

  • Adaptive tuning (client): adjust simulation distance as performance changes, with optional entity-distance and particle fallbacks. Explore adaptive tuning
  • Calmer crowds (world): throttle distant mob AI and merge nearby item drops and XP orbs through independent settings. Explore world features
  • Quieter bursts (comfort): set budgets for sounds and particles, and save power when the game window is unfocused. Explore comfort features

Less background strain. More room to play.

Minecraft has a lot going on at once. Chlorine focuses on the parts you can tune, without trying to replace the systems that already do their job well.

  • Simulation that can scale. Give the world a little breathing room when frames dip, then let it recover when things settle.
  • Distant activity, handled gently. Throttle distant mob decision-making while leaving nearby mobs and their physics alone.
  • Effects with a sensible budget. Keep bursts of sounds and particles from piling up all at once, one optional setting at a time.
  • Power saving when you step away. Lower the FPS cap while the game is unfocused, with an option to hide clouds too.
  • Steadier frame times. Spread light-update bursts and the periodic autosave over several frames, and skip far-away detail you can't see anyway.

Your game. Your settings. Your call.

  1. Install. Add Chlorine to your Fabric instance alongside Fabric API and Cloth Config.
  2. Choose. Open the config screen or edit the JSON file. Every feature can be switched off.
  3. Fine-tune. Start with the defaults and adjust gradually for your world and hardware.

A few quick answers.

Does Chlorine change render distance?

No. Its adaptive tuning works with simulation distance; render distance is intentionally left alone to avoid expensive chunk render graph rebuilds.

Do I need another performance mod?

For Chlorine 1.0.3+ on Minecraft 26.3, Sodium 0.9.2 or newer is required because the workload governor integrates with Sodium's renderer. Lithium, FerriteCore, EntityCulling, ImmediatelyFast, and Iris remain optional.

Will it improve performance in every world?

Results depend on your hardware, world, entity count, and mod setup. Chlorine provides configurable tools, not a universal FPS guarantee.
